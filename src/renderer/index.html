How to read it

  • Brightness is loudness. Height is frequency (low at the bottom), left to right is time.
  • Real lossless (a proper FLAC, WAV or AIFF from a CD or a lossless download) has sound up to about 20–22 kHz.
  • A hard horizontal edge with black above it means an MP3 or AAC was in the chain: the encoder throws away everything above a set frequency. About 16–17 kHz is a 128 kbps MP3, about 18.5 kHz 192 kbps, about 20 kHz 256–320 kbps. Converting that MP3 to FLAC afterwards can't bring the top back.
  • Limits: some high-bitrate MP3s (V0) and AACs don't leave a hard edge, so a few fakes pass as real; and some CD masters are filtered near 20 kHz, so that range is only ever "possibly".
  • Hi-res (88.2 kHz and up) is a separate question: turn on Settings → Audio Analyzer → Also check hi-res claims to have it checked too.
  • Tempo and key come from the whole track. The BPM matched rekordbox's within 0.1 for about 8 tracks in 10 in a test on a techno library. The key is an estimate: it agreed with rekordbox about half the time, and "no clear key" means the track gives too little to go on. Use on Camelot wheel takes both to the deck.
  • Compare two copies of the same track, e.g. a store download and a rip, to see which one holds more. Their spectrograms share one frequency scale.

How the maths works

  • Pitch change in semitones = 12 × log2(1 + tempo% / 100). So +6% ≈ +1.01 semitones and −6% ≈ −1.07.
  • One semitone up moves you 7 steps clockwise on the Camelot wheel (1A → 8A). One semitone down moves 7 steps the other way (1A → 6A). Letters A/B never change.
  • Two semitones up is +2 on the wheel (1A → 3A). That's why a +2 key change sounds like an energy boost.
  • About ±3% is half a semitone: the track sits between two keys and clashes with both. Small moves of ±1–2% only detune it slightly.
  • Master Tempo (key lock) keeps the original key whatever the tempo. Turn it off and the key moves as shown here.
  • Detune guide: within 10 cents counts as in key. 10–25 cents is slightly off and often fine on drum-heavy parts. Over 25 cents is noticeably out of tune.
